Classroom Experiments

Instructor guide: setting up the class, approving designs, running the live session and collecting the data

Groups of students design randomized experiments as surveys; on class day each group presents while everyone answers its survey on their phones and the results appear on the projected dashboard. This guide covers everything on the instructor side. Students have their own guide (docs/student-guide.pdf).

1. Creating the classroom

On the site's home page, choose Create Classroom and give it:

  • a code students type to join (e.g. ECON101; unique, not case-sensitive);
  • a host password, only for you: it opens the instructor pages;
  • a classroom password that you share with the class (it's also in the QR code on the live dashboard);
  • the roster: a CSV with the students' IDs and names. A Canvas gradebook export works as is.

Later, log in from Host a Classroom on the home page with the code and the host password. Students sign in with their SIS ID; the first time they confirm their first name and choose a password, so names and IDs in your data always match the roster.

2. The instructor home

Everything between classes is on the instructor home: the class phase, the roster, the surveys and the data. Start live session opens the page you project in class.

  • Max questions per survey (Surveys card): students can't save more; you're exempt.
  • Reminders and part 2 until: the latest date any group's notifications or remote parts can fall on. Leave it empty for no limit.
  • Two-part dates and reminders to approve lists groups waiting for you (see section 6).
  • Survey answers collected counts each student who answered a survey once. It stays at 0 until surveys run (in class, launched on their own, opened early or a remote part), however many surveys groups have built.

3. The roster

Manage roster shows every student, whether they signed up, their group, its size and any pending invites. The filters at the top narrow the list: name or ID, signed up or not, in a group or not, with a pending invite, alone in their group, or group size fewer than, exactly or more than a number of members. "Showing X of Y" counts what's left.

  • Upload again any time: new IDs are added, existing students keep their passwords and groups. Optionally remove students who aren't in the file.
  • Add one student for late additions; Reset password if someone forgets theirs (they choose a new one on their next sign-in); Remove hides a student who dropped (their past answers stay in your data; you can restore them).
  • Max members per group and Max groups per student: lowering them never removes anyone; full groups just can't take more.

4. Class phases

The Class phase card moves the class through four steps with Next step, or with individual switches:

PhaseWhat students can do
BuildingCreate groups, invite teammates, build and edit surveys.
LockedSurveys and groups are frozen (you can still edit). Each student sees the groups they'll comment on. Previews and downloads keep working.
Feedback openStudents send their comments on other groups (each group opens for comments once its survey has run).
Feedback closedComments are final; groups read the comments they received, anonymized.

Set Required comments per student before locking; assignments are balanced so every group gets about the same number of reviewers, never their own.

5. Managing surveys

Manage surveys lists every group's survey with its members (count, "alone" and "full" badges, pending invites), status and badges (not finished, two-part, waiting for your approval). Filter by title or member, by number of members, or by status.

  • Edit opens a group's survey page; you can always edit (saving a survey that has responses deletes them, after a warning).
  • Allow early lets a one-part survey run before class: the group submits it with an answer-by date, classmates answer from their lobby, and it still runs in class. Copy email text gives you an announcement to paste.
  • Running it shows how each survey (or each part) runs and whether it already ran:
    • Launch opens the live dashboard with only that survey or part, e.g. a part that runs at the start of class, or a group that presents on another day.
    • Next Survey in the live session skips everything that already ran.
    • Run again… (host password) deletes that survey's responses and notification sign-ups so it runs again as new, for a group that changed its survey after running it. Download its data first.
  • View survey (from a survey's page or the lobby) shows all arms side by side; the flow chart shows each arm's path.

6. Two-part surveys and notifications

Both are optional; by default a survey has one part, no notifications, and runs in the live session. Groups set them up in two cards at the bottom of their survey page.

Two-part surveys

Part 2 starts at a question the group chooses. Each part runs in the live session, in class, launched separately (by you, from Manage surveys), or remote (answered from the lobby between the group's dates). Part 2 is only for students who answered part 1, in the same arm; in the live session, others see "This survey is for classmates who answered its part 1" and wait for the next survey. The dashboard marks part-2 questions.

Notifications

Per arm: a short note respondents read first, then messages, each with a time of day and chosen days. After answering (part 1), respondents add them to their calendar (iPhone and computers: a calendar file; Android: Google Calendar links) or turn on notifications from the site. Times are each phone's local time unless the group picks a time zone.

Your approval

Notifications and remote dates need your approval: nothing is sent, and remote parts stay closed, until you approve. Review them on the group's View survey page, where they're listed under the questions with an Approve button. If the group changes them afterwards, they need approval again (the home page lists them).

Push notifications on the server need nothing set up: the site creates its own keys on first use and sends due notifications every minute. Optionally set the environment variable VAPID_SUBJECT on Render to mailto: plus your email, as a contact for the push services. iPhones only get site notifications if the site is added to the Home Screen, so iPhone users are offered the calendar.

7. The demo dashboard

Demo dashboard (Surveys card on the home page) lets you click through every survey's questions, images and charts with made-up answers, without going live. Use it to check the surveys before class or to walk the class through the designs.

8. Class day: the live session

  1. On the home page, click Start live session and project it. Students join from their lobby, or by scanning the QR code with the classroom code and password.
  2. Click a survey on the left, or Next Survey, to start it. Students' phones show it within a few seconds; each student always gets the same arm.
  3. Click Refresh Results for the latest charts (they don't update on their own, to keep the site fast with a full class).
  4. Next Survey moves on in group order, skipping surveys or parts that already ran; Reset Session stops the current survey (nothing is deleted).
  • Two-part surveys show which part runs here ("part 2"); a "✓ ran" badge marks what already ran (click it to show its results again).
  • Block designers from own survey shows group members "You designed this survey" instead of their own questions.
  • Surveys run on another platform are listed for the order but don't start here.

9. Peer feedback

When you lock surveys, each student is assigned groups to comment on. Comments are drafts until sent; students can jot private notes right after answering a live survey. Groups read the comments as "Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2"; you see names. Feedback comments and Feedback summary (required sent per student) are in the Data card for grading.

10. Data and downloads

Download everything (.zip) on the home page has every file plus the uploaded images:

FileContents
all_responses.csvEvery answer, with names, IDs, the respondent ID, arm, time on the question, left-page and timeout flags, and part.
surveys_config.csvWhat each arm showed: question texts and options.
participants.csvThe roster key: names, IDs and the random respondent IDs students see.
survey_designers.csv / survey_participation.csvWho designed each survey; who answered each one.
feedback.csv / feedback_summary.csvComments and required-comment counts, for grading.
reminder_plans.csvEach group's notifications per arm, how its parts run, and approval.
reminder_actions.csv / reminder_notifications.csvWho downloaded the calendar file, opened Google Calendar links, turned notifications on; each notification sent, delivered and tapped.

Groups download their own survey's responses, anonymized (random respondent IDs only), in wide (one row per respondent) or long (one row per answer) format.

11. Deploying and backups

  • The site runs on Render with a persistent disk (/data) for the database and images. Pushing to the repository deploys it.
  • Before deploying, download the zip, and if possible a copy of /data/app.db. Don't deploy during class: the site restarts.
  • Updates only add to the database; existing classrooms, students, groups and surveys are kept.

12. Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
A student can't sign inCheck they're on the roster (not removed); reset their password if needed.
A survey doesn't appear on phonesStudents poll every 3 seconds; have them go back to the lobby and click Join. Check the survey isn't a part that's remote, or that it isn't already "ran" (click it to show it).
A group needs to run its survey againManage surveys → Run again… (download its data first).
A remote part doesn't openIt needs your approval and its dates; see "to approve" on the home page.
