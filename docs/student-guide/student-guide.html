Classroom Experiments

Student guide: building, running and reviewing your group's experiment

Your group designs a small randomized experiment as a survey, runs it on your classmates in class, gives feedback on other groups' experiments, and analyzes your own results. Everything happens on one site, on a laptop or a phone.

  1. Sign in with the classroom code, the classroom password and your student ID.
  2. Form your group and build your survey. One person creates it and invites the rest.
  3. Optional extras, only if your design needs them: follow-up questions, timers, a second part answered later, notifications.
  4. Preview it as a respondent and as the results screen, and fix any warnings.
  5. Your instructor locks all surveys before class. You can still preview and download.
  6. Class day: answer every other group's survey on your phone while each group presents, in the presentation order.
  7. Give feedback to the groups you're assigned, plus any others you like.
  8. Download your responses (anonymized) and analyze them.

1. Joining and signing in

You sign in with your SIS User ID and a password you choose the first time. Your name comes from the class roster, so there's nothing to type and no typos in the data used for grading.

1

Go to the site and click Join Classroom, or scan the QR code shown in class. Enter the classroom code and the classroom password your instructor gave you, then click Join.

2

Enter your SIS User ID and click Continue.

3

The first time, the site asks "Are you [your first name]?" Click Yes, that's me. If it shows someone else's name, click No and check your ID. Never sign in as a classmate.

4

Choose a password (at least 6 characters) and type it twice. A green check shows when both match. Click Save and continue. Next time, you sign in with your SIS User ID and this password.

"ID not found"? Check your ID and try again. If it still fails, email your instructor: you may not be on the roster yet.

3. Forming your group

Only one person per group creates the group; everyone else waits for an invite. You don't need to have the survey ready: form the group first, then build the survey together. Each student can be in one group, and a group can have at most 5 members (unless your instructor says otherwise).

1

One member clicks Create your group in the lobby, types a title (optional) and, under Teammates, starts typing each classmate's name, picks it from the list and clicks Add. Then Create group & start building. They're added to the group automatically, and the group gets its number automatically. That number is your place in the presentation order.

2

This opens your survey page. More invites can be sent any time from its Group Members card, at the top.

3

The other members accept the invite in their lobby, under Group invites.

Once you accept, your group appears under My group:

"You can't accept this"? Either that group is already full, or you're already in another group. In the second case, click Leave next to your current group in your lobby first. The last member of a group can't leave; invite someone first or delete the survey. Once your instructor locks surveys and groups, membership can't change.

4. Designing your survey

A survey is one experiment: each respondent is randomly shown one arm (control or a treatment), and the arms differ only in what you want to test. The survey page is made of cards (Group Members, Survey details, Arms, Questions, and two optional ones at the bottom). Click a card's title, or a question's, to fold it away; the ⓘ icons explain each setting.

1

Arms (up to 4). Name them, for example "Control" and "High anchor". Each respondent sees exactly one arm, and always the same one.

2

Questions. Every arm has the same questions, but the wording, image and answer options can differ per arm. That difference is your treatment. Your instructor sets a maximum number of questions (shown next to Questions); short surveys get better answers.

3

Copy from Control. Each arm after the first has this link. It copies the first arm's text, options and image, so you only change the part that differs.

4

Folded questions show a one-line summary with colored badges for any extras (only in some arms, follow-up, timer, "Other", part 2). Drag a question by its ⠿ handle to move it up or down: no need to delete and retype.

5

Images (PNG or JPEG, up to 5 MB) are optional, per arm and question. + Add "Other" adds an "Other" choice with a text box to a multiple-choice question.

6

Warnings. The page warns you if two arms are identical in every question, which means there's no treatment.

Example. To test anchoring: two arms, one numeric question. Control asks "How much would you pay for this mug?" The "High anchor" arm asks "Most students pay $25 for this mug. How much would you pay?"

Saving. Your edits save automatically, even when the survey isn't finished: the bar at the top says when. A blue Not finished yet box lists what's still missing (for example "Question 1, Treatment: write the question"), and your lobby shows Not finished until it's complete. Discard all changes from this session puts the survey back the way it was when you opened it in that tab. Teammates editing at the same time overwrite each other, so take turns.

Getting around. Use My lobby in the top bar (or the "← My lobby" link) to go back, and Sign out when you're done on a shared computer.

Group members. The top of the survey page lists your group and lets you invite more classmates.

5. Advanced options and earlier answers

Most surveys don't need any of this. Each question has an ⚙ Advanced options button that opens:

  • Shown in: untick an arm to ask the question only in the other arms (its version in the unticked arm is greyed out).
  • Show it: only if Question N is …: a follow-up, shown only to respondents who picked certain answers to an earlier multiple-choice question. Everyone else skips it.
  • Timer, per arm: a time limit in seconds (up to 600), shown as a countdown, a shrinking bar, both, or hidden; and the answer recorded if time runs out (or none). The data marks timed-out answers.

Putting an earlier answer into a later question

Type {{Q1}} in a question's text to show the respondent's answer to Question 1 there ({{Q2}} for Question 2, and so on; upper or lower case both work). For example, Question 2's text You said ${{Q1}}. Would you buy the mug at that price today? appears to someone who answered 30 as:

Rules for {{Qn}}: the number is the question's number on your survey page, it must be an earlier question, and that question must be answered in the same sitting (in a two-part survey, both questions in the same part). If the earlier question was skipped (a follow-up the respondent didn't get), they see the text {{Q1}} itself, so only use answers everyone in that arm gives. Multiple-answer questions show the choices separated by commas, and "Other" shows what the respondent typed. Check it with Preview survey.

6. Two-part surveys (optional)

Use this only if you need two rounds of answers from the same classmates, for example a baseline and then an outcome after a week of notifications. Open the Two-part survey card under your questions and turn on This survey has two parts.

  • Part 2 starts at: the questions before it are part 1. A dashed line marks the break on the page.
  • For each part, choose when it's answered: in class, during the live session (when you present; the default for part 1), in class, launched separately by your instructor (for example at the start of class), or remote: classmates answer from their lobby, on their own time, between the dates you choose. Both parts can't be in the live session, since they'd follow each other right away.
  • Part 2 is only for classmates who answered part 1, and they stay in the same arm.
  • A part-2 question can't be a follow-up to a part-1 answer, and {{Qn}} doesn't work across parts.
  • Your instructor approves remote dates before they open. Changing them later needs approval again.

7. Notifications (optional)

Notifications (nudges) are messages respondents get on their phones after answering your survey (part 1 of a two-part survey), for example "Time for a glass of water!" every day at noon for a week. They work with one-part surveys too. Each arm gets its own, or none.

  • What respondents read before turning them on, per arm: e.g. "You'll get a notification about drinking water every day at noon this week."
  • + Add notification for each message, with its own time of day and days: tap days on the calendar, or use Quick fill (every day, every 2 or 3 days, every week, or weekdays only, between two dates). Add several to send a different message in week 2, or the same message at different times.
  • Times are in each phone's own time by default (noon is noon wherever the respondent is), or in a time zone you pick.
  • Respondents choose how to get them: their calendar, or notifications from the site. Your instructor approves them before anyone gets them.
A placebo helps. If only your treatment arm gets notifications, only that arm goes through the "add them" step, so the arms differ in more than the message. Consider a neutral message for the control arm on the same days.

8. Running your survey on another platform

With your instructor's permission, your group can run its survey somewhere else, such as Google Forms. In that case your group randomizes the versions and emails them to classmates; this site won't run your survey.

1

Create your group as usual (so the group and its members are registered), and give the survey a title.

2

Turn on We're running our survey on another platform. The arms and questions disappear and are no longer required.

3

Optionally, describe how you're running it. Classmates see this note when they give you feedback.

You keep your place in the presentation order, present in your turn and get peer feedback like every other group. There are no live results in class, so bring your own. Anything you'd already built here is kept: turning the switch off brings it back.

9. Previewing before class

Check your survey from both sides before it's locked. Nothing you do in a preview is saved.

1

Preview survey (top of the survey page) shows exactly what a respondent sees on their phone, for the arm (and part) you pick. Switch arms with the buttons at the top. After part 1 of a two-part survey, a Break between the parts card shows how part 2 runs, the notifications that arm gets (with Send me a test notification, to see one on your own phone), and Continue to part 2.

2

Preview dashboard shows the charts and tables that will be projected in class, filled with made-up responses. Change the sample size, or click New random sample to see how results vary. Download example answers gives the same made-up answers in the format of your real data, so you can prepare your analysis before class.

3

View as flow chart draws each arm's path through your survey: the questions it sees, follow-ups (yes/no), timers, and the break between parts. Click a question to jump to it.

10. Presentation order

Your lobby lists every group's survey in the order they run in class, with each group's members. Your group is highlighted, so you know when you present. Groups running their survey on another platform are marked Other platform: no live results; they still present in their turn. The order can change until your instructor locks surveys.

11. Class day

Bring a charged phone or laptop. Sign in before class starts so you're ready.

1

In your lobby, click Join on the Live Session card. You'll see "Waiting for the next survey".

2

When the instructor starts a group's survey, it appears on your screen within a few seconds. Answer and click Submit All Answers.

3

Wait for the next one. Keep the page open; there's no need to refresh.

Notes for feedback. After you submit, a small link appears: Add a note for your feedback (optional). Jot down your thoughts while they're fresh. Only you can see the note, and it becomes your draft comment for that group.

Your own group's survey: you may see "You designed this survey" instead of the questions; designers usually don't answer their own experiment. If you see "Your answer was not saved", tap Submit again.

Notifications after answering. If the group set up notifications, a Your notifications box appears after you submit. On an iPhone, tap Add to my calendar, then Add All. On Android, tap each Add button and then Save in Google Calendar, or tap Get notifications on this device instead and allow notifications. On a computer, download the calendar file. You can add them later from your lobby too.

Parts answered on your own time. Remote parts of two-part surveys appear in your lobby with Answer part 1 or Answer part 2 while they're open. Part 2 only appears if you answered part 1.

12. Peer feedback

Part of your grade is commenting on other groups' experiments: at least the groups you're assigned, marked required. Groups are assigned at random so every group gets about the same number of reviewers, and never your own.

  • You'll see your assigned groups as soon as surveys are locked, before comments open. Pay close attention to their presentations in class.
  • A group opens for comments once its survey has run, in class or early from Open surveys, and stays open until comments close. Until then its card says you can comment once it runs, so you answer every survey before seeing how it works.
  • View survey shows all of a group's arms side by side, so you can compare what each arm saw. For a group that ran its survey on another platform, it shows their description instead.
  • Save draft is private. Notes you took in class appear here as drafts.
  • Send works once your instructor opens comments. You can keep editing a sent comment until comments close.
  • Comment on another group adds extra comments; you can remove extras, but not required ones.
  • The counter ("1 of 2 required sent") shows your progress.

Comments are anonymous to the groups: they see "Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2", while your instructor sees names for grading. Groups read their comments only after comments close. Good comments are specific: what works, and one concrete change to the design, wording or treatment.

13. Downloading your data

After your survey runs, download its responses from your lobby (the number in the Responses column) or from the bottom of the survey page. Downloads keep working after surveys are locked.

The file is a CSV that opens in Excel, Google Sheets, R or Stata. Wide (the default) has one row per respondent and one column per question, plus time on each question, whether they left the page, and timeouts:

respondent_id,arm,Q1 Willingness to pay (numeric),...
R-7KQ2PX,Control,8,...
R-M4TW9A,High anchor,20,...

Long has one row per respondent and question (respondent, arm, part, question, answer, seconds...), handy for regressions with question or part effects. Two-part surveys mark part-2 questions, and surveys with notifications add whether each respondent added them (calendar file, Google Calendar links, notifications turned on, delivered and tapped).

Respondents appear only as random IDs, never by name. Compare the answers across arms: that difference is your treatment effect. The Survey Config download lists what each arm showed. Groups that ran their survey on another platform collect their own data there.
